# Starter code review

I compared the original `app/main.py` with the API contract and data model. The table records the defects addressed in
the submitted implementation and the observable behavior that exposed each one.

| # | Where (function / line) | What was wrong | How to notice it | How it was fixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly employee analytics | The employee/month summary required by the contract was not implemented. | `GET /analytics/employees/EMP0001/monthly?month=2026-07` did not return the required summary. | Added `build_employee_monthly_pipeline` and `employee_monthly`, including join-date working days and stored attendance metrics. |
| 2 | Late leaderboard | Monthly late totals, ranking, and tie behavior were missing. | The leaderboard route was unavailable or could not include all employees tied at the rank cutoff. | Added `build_late_leaderboard_pipeline` and competition ranking; the rank limit is applied after ranking. |
| 3 | Department trend | The contract requires one row per calendar date, including dates without logs. | A date range with no attendance record for a day could not produce the required daily row. | Added `build_department_trend_pipeline` with date gap filling, daily headcount, rates, and a seven-calendar-row moving average. |
| 4 | Explain endpoint | There was no endpoint to inspect the plans of the specified queries. | Requests to `/admin/explain/{endpoint}` could not return MongoDB execution statistics. | Added the endpoint for the five contract names; it builds the corresponding query or pipeline and calls `explain` with `executionStats`. |
| 5 | Department summary rounding | Rounding an aggregate mean in MongoDB could use ties-to-even, contrary to the contract's half-up rule. | A mean exactly halfway at two decimal places could round down where half-up requires rounding up. | The aggregation retains the sum and count; `department_summary` rounds the calculated mean with `Decimal` and `ROUND_HALF_UP`. |
| 6 | `EmployeeIn` validation | Employee fields, calendar dates, shift times, and different start/end times were not all checked against the request contract. | Invalid codes, impossible dates, malformed times, or equal shift times could be submitted. | Added field constraints, strict `YYYY-MM-DD` validation, and a model-level check that shift times differ. |
| 7 | Epoch-millisecond request fields | Coercible integer fields did not guarantee that timestamps were actual bounded epoch-millisecond integers. | Boolean, fractional, string, seconds-based, or out-of-range timestamp input could be accepted or mishandled. | Added strict integer validation and the documented lower and upper bounds for punch and correction timestamps. |
| 8 | Employee monthly attendance rate | The reported rate did not use the required four-decimal rate precision. | For example, `0.5 / 3 * 100` should be reported as `16.6667`, not `16.67`. | `employee_monthly` uses decimal half-up rounding to four places for `attendance_pct`. |
| 9 | Regularization compare-and-update | Legacy attendance documents may omit `history`; requiring an empty history array prevented those records from being updated. | A correction to a record without `history` could incorrectly return 409. | `regularize_attendance` matches a missing history field with `$exists: false` and still checks the record's current values before updating. |
| 10 | Month parsing | Constructing the next month could overflow at the upper calendar boundary. | A request for `month=9999-12` could produce a server error instead of a validation response. | `parse_month` validates the month and returns 422 when its next-month boundary cannot be represented. |
| 11 | Response serialization | BSON dates, MongoDB `_id`, and optional legacy attendance fields did not consistently match the API response contract. | Responses could expose an internal ID, omit required nullable/default fields, or return timestamps in the wrong representation. | `serialize_mongo_datetimes` and `serialize_attendance_record` convert instants to epoch milliseconds, remove `_id`, and supply documented defaults. |
| 12 | Punch-out route | The starter did not complete the punch-out workflow required to close an open attendance record. | A present employee could punch in but could not record an end time and receive computed end-of-day fields. | Added `POST /attendance/punch-out`: it finds the most recent punch-in at or before the requested time, rejects an already-closed record and invalid or over-24-hour intervals, computes derived fields, and atomically updates only an open record. |
| 13 | Regularization route | Manual attendance corrections and their audit history were not implemented to the contract. | A manager could not correct status or punch times while seeing exactly what changed. | Added `PATCH /attendance/{emp_code}/{date}` validation, derived-field recalculation, no-op rejection, concurrency checking, and an append-only history entry containing only changed fields. |
| 14 | Attendance calculations | Attendance rules require whole-second instants, IST shift dates, strict grace/threshold boundaries, and decimal half-up rounding. | Boundary cases such as `09:40:01`, a punch just below 30 minutes overtime, or an overnight shift could yield incorrect derived values. | Added the IST-aware punch-date handling and the `compute_late_minutes`, `compute_overtime`, and `compute_work_hours` helpers; half-day is based on rounded work hours. |
| 15 | Startup database indexes | Application-level checks alone cannot prevent concurrent duplicate employee or attendance inserts; date and join-date analytics also need indexed access paths. | Concurrent punch-ins could create duplicate daily records, or the required queries could lack their intended indexes. | The FastAPI lifespan creates unique `emp_code` and `(emp_code, date)` indexes, plus attendance date/sort and employee join-date/department indexes. |
| 16 | Attendance-list query validation | Date filters need strict calendar-date validation and reversed ranges must be rejected; query results also need contract pagination and sorting. | Malformed dates or a `date_from` later than `date_to` could produce invalid filters, while unbounded or unsorted results would not match the API contract. | `parse_calendar_date` and `build_attendance_query` validate dates and ranges; `list_attendance` applies the requested filters, count, sort, and bounded pagination in MongoDB. |

## Checked and not considered defects

- The assignment's single-file application layout is intentional; all API implementation remains in `app/main.py`.
- BSON dates in MongoDB and epoch milliseconds in API requests and responses are the required storage and transport
  formats, not a mismatch.
- The supplied sample records illustrate document shapes only. Analytics also need to account for weekends, join dates,
  employees without logs, ranking ties, and dates with no records, as addressed by the contract.
